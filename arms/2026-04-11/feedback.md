## 4. Form Notes

**Diamond Push-ups — "For warm-up triceps. Today's bodyweight"**
Fine. This is appropriate activation work. Keep it genuinely easy — if you're grinding reps here, you're burning fuel before the session starts. 8 reps at BW is reasonable.

**Dips — "Plus 10kg weight plates added"**
Good. Weighted dips are the right progression step. Make sure the weight plate is secured (belt/chain) not just balanced — "plus 10kg weight plates" (plural) is ambiguous. Clarify your setup next log.

**Straight Bar Pushdown — "Drop some weight for better mind muscle connection"**
Correct call. Going from 50kg to 45kg for the third set to reconnect is valid, especially late in a session. Just don't make this a habit as a workaround for going too heavy too early.

**Bayesian Curls — "I went to failure" / "Went to failure"**
This is the session's biggest execution mistake. Going to failure on set 1 and set 2 of your opening bicep movement is how you blow your subsequent bicep work. Your set 3 dropped to 7 reps (vs. 12) — that's the direct consequence. Failure should be reserved for the **last set** of an exercise, not used as a rep target from the start. Take sets 1 and 2 to RPE 8–9 (leave 1–2 reps in the tank).

**Hammer Curls — "Right arm only 6 reps. Had to spot myself for other 2 reps" / "Had to spot myself for last rep. Both arms"**
Self-spotting on hammer curls = ego load or extreme fatigue. At 10kg, this is pure fatigue accumulation from the Bayesian failure sets. This is not a technique or weight problem — it's sequencing and fatigue management. You should never need to spot yourself on a 10kg dumbbell hammer curl unless you've completely depleted the muscle before getting there.

**Dead Hangs — No reps/duration logged**
Log your hold time. Even a simple "3 × 20s" gives trackable data. This matters for grip strength baseline and decompression tracking.
