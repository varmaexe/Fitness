## 4. Form Notes

**Glutes Hyperextension — "Feeling some in lower back":** You're extending past parallel or leading with spinal extension rather than glute contraction. On this machine for glutes, the pad should sit at hip crease level, not mid-thigh. Drive through your heel, squeeze your glute hard at the top, and stop at parallel — not beyond. The cue is: *the glute squeeze ends the rep, not the spine lifting.* If the lower back is lighting up before the glutes, you're hyperextending.

**Smith Hack Squats — "Felt pain on left knee while going up" (40kg, 2 reps):** Pain on the concentric phase of a squatting movement = patellar tendon stress or lateral tracking issue. The Smith machine's fixed vertical bar path is the problem — it doesn't allow your body to track naturally and forces a mechanically compromised position for your knee. You correctly stopped at 2 reps. Good.

**Smith Hack Squats sets 2 & 3 — "Went ahead slowly without jerks":** Wrong decision. When a movement caused you to stop at 2 reps from pain in set 1, the correct response is to rack it and move to a different exercise — not to slow the tempo and try again. Slow tempo reduces impact forces but doesn't remove the pain cause. You essentially loaded an already-painful joint for 16 more reps. Next time: pain in set 1 on a compound = end the exercise.

**Smith Hack Squats set 3 — "Slight tightness in lower back":** You now have knee pain AND lower back activation on this exercise. That's two signals simultaneously. The Smith machine forces a fixed plane on a movement your body is trying to adjust organically, creating spinal compensation. This exercise needs to come out of rotation until the knee is clear.

**Leg Press — "Feet in middle → wider = less knee pain":** Correct finding, correct adjustment. Wide stance reduces the knee's valgus load and distributes force through the hip extensors more. Keep feet high and wide as your default until the knee is pain-free. Mid-plate, narrow stance is a quad-emphasis variation — not appropriate right now.

**Leg Extension — "Careful but little to no knee pain":** Positive, but stay vigilant at terminal extension (last 20–30°). If sharp pain appears at lockout, limit ROM to 0–70° and don't lock out. The "good burn in all sets" at 40kg×12 suggests the patellar stress is tolerable here — but if Smith hacks are causing pain and you're doing leg extension right after, the cumulative load matters.

**Landmine Single-Leg RDL — no form note logged:** With single-leg RDL, the common failure is hip rotation/drop on the working side. Keep your hips square, trace the weight down your shin, and don't let the floating hip hike up. If you don't have a mirror, do these near a wall to feel for hip drift.
